Throughout the last two years, I was incredibly fortunate to meet
and work with many tremendous people, from students who have similar interests
as me to experts in our field of optimization who were willing to share and
discuss interesting problems.
The work of my Master's thesis wouldn't have been possible without all the
mentorship I received early on, as well as the collaboration opportunities
I received later on.
I would like to take this opportunity to give some regards to everyone who
has been a part of this journey along with me.

During my Master's, I received funding from my advisor,
and also received a scholarship to do a visit at Stanford University.
I am extremely thankful to Prof. Antoine Lesage-Landry, Maxime Fortin and the Bourse Prestige
scholarship program at Polytechnique Montréal for these opportunities.
I am also thankful to Anthropic's open source program which gave me free
access to their latest language models; I must admit these models have been
used extensively (as an assistant) throughout all parts of my recent work
from research, to coding and even writing.

I must naturally begin by thanking my advisor Prof. Antoine Lesage-Landry,
for taking a chance on an inexperienced and somewhat troublesome student like I was
two years ago (and definitely still am today).
Throughout our many meetings, Antoine showed a lot of patience in explaining
complicated proofs, almost unlimited optimism when faced with research roadblocks
and overall charisma which have all positively affected me.
I would also like to thank Prof. Sébastien Le
Digabel and Prof. Antoine Legrain for being part of my jury.

I was also very fortunate to work with Prof. Stephen Boyd, which

I cannot express my gratitude enough for the many members of the open-source optimization
