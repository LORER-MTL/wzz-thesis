\Chapter{BENCHMARK RESULTS}\label{sec:Theme2}

This chapter presents benchmark results which compare the \texttt{diffengine} backend
(the \texttt{CVXPY} implementation based on \texttt{SparseDiffEngine}) against
\texttt{CVXPY}'s own canonicalization backends: the default \texttt{cvxcore} backend, the
matrix-stuffing baseline of \S\ref{sec:matrix-stuffing}, and the two pure-Python backends,
\texttt{SCIPY} and \texttt{COO}, that \texttt{CVXPY} ships as alternatives to it. The
comparison is restricted to \texttt{CVXPY}'s own backends
because that is the claim under test: \texttt{diffengine} is a drop-in replacement for the
canonicalization backend, and the relevant question is whether it constructs the same
problem data faster.
All backends run on the $25$ problems of \texttt{CVXPY}'s public benchmark suite
(\url{https://github.com/cvxpy/benchmarks}). These convex optimization problems cover many
fields, ranging from portfolio models, robust regression, and semidefinite programs to simple
LPs and QPs that are close to standard form.

\section{Benchmark Setup}
\label{sec:bench-setup}

Every benchmark measures the construction of the problem data $(A,b,c,P)$; no solver is
invoked. For all backends this corresponds to a call to \texttt{get\_problem\_data}
with the target solver fixed to \texttt{Clarabel}~\cite{clarabel}, and that single call is
the entire timed region. The \texttt{SCIPY} and \texttt{COO} backends are selected through
the \texttt{canon\_backend} argument of the same call, and \texttt{diffengine} by passing
\texttt{ignore\_dpp=True}.

On the parametric problems the default path does not build the same object as
\texttt{diffengine}. \texttt{CVXPY}'s \emph{disciplined parametrized programming} (DPP)
path builds a parameter-to-data tensor, while \texttt{diffengine} emits the concrete
matrices directly. Every parametric measurement is therefore also taken with
\texttt{ignore\_dpp=True} on \texttt{CVXPY}'s backends, on which path each parameter is
substituted by its value and the backend builds the same matrices \texttt{diffengine}
does.

\texttt{CVXPY} also does not always use the backend it is asked for. When no
\texttt{canon\_backend} is given and the total parameter size reaches $1000$ entries, it
silently substitutes \texttt{COO} for \texttt{cvxcore}. The \texttt{CPP} column is
therefore obtained by passing \texttt{canon\_backend="CPP"} explicitly.

Each cold construction is measured in a fresh process, single-threaded, on a 2018 MacBook
Pro. The parametric measurements of \texttt{CVXPY}'s backends require unmodified
\texttt{CVXPY}, so they are taken in a second environment running \texttt{CVXPY} 1.9.2 as
released. That environment is slightly
faster than the one carrying the \texttt{diffengine} fork, so every comparison that
crosses the two is conservative, working against \texttt{diffengine}.
The benchmark scripts and all recorded timings are available in the
\texttt{canonicalization/} directory of the \texttt{Jacobian-accumulation}
repository\footnote{\url{https://github.com/SparseDifferentiation/Jacobian-accumulation}};
every ratio quoted in this chapter is reproducible from the committed result files.

\section{Non-parametric problems}
\label{sec:bench-nonparam}

On the $17$ problems whose data are constants no parameter tensor exists, so all four
systems construct the same object and the comparison needs no adjustment
(Table~\ref{tab:cold-compile}). \texttt{cvxcore} is frequently not the fastest of
\texttt{CVXPY}'s own backends; the pure-Python \texttt{SCIPY} backend beats it on $14$ of
the $17$ problems. The ratio is therefore taken against the fastest of the three on each
problem. Against \texttt{cvxcore} alone the geometric mean
would be $0.20$ rather than $0.32$, a difference attributable to the choice of baseline
rather than to \texttt{diffengine}.

\texttt{diffengine} is the fastest of the four on $14$ of the $17$ problems, with a
geometric-mean ratio of $0.32$, that is, $3.1\times$ faster than an oracle that picks the
best \texttt{CVXPY} backend for each problem. The largest margins occur where the
baselines' per-expression tensor construction dominates, as in the portfolio and
regression models with many scalar constraints; \textsc{UnconstrainedQP}, a tiny problem
wrapped in a large \texttt{kron} expression, is the extreme case at $121\times$ against
the best backend ($146\times$ against \texttt{cvxcore}).

The three exceptions share a mechanism. \textsc{CVaR} ($1.01$),
\textsc{OptimalAdvertising} ($1.05$) and \textsc{SDPSegfault1132} ($1.39$) are the
problems whose compilation is dominated by moving one large block of constant data into
the problem matrices rather than by computing coefficients. Where there are no
coefficients to derive, structural knowledge of the atoms buys nothing, and the comparison
reduces to how efficiently each system copies memory.

\section{Parametric problems}
\label{sec:bench-param}

On the $8$ parametric problems \texttt{CVXPY}'s backends are measured on the
\texttt{ignore\_dpp} path, so that all four systems construct the same matrices
(Table~\ref{tab:param-cold}). The DPP path is not a usable baseline here. Building the
parameter-to-data tensor costs \texttt{cvxcore} and \texttt{SCIPY} $18$--$60\times$ their
direct construction time on the problems with tens of thousands of parameter entries, and
on the two largest, \textsc{SimpleFullyParametrizedLP} and \textsc{ParametrizedQP}, both
backends fail to complete at all. Only \texttt{COO}, whose coordinate-format tensor
scales with the number of stored nonzeros rather than with the parameter dimension
(\S\ref{sec:bg-sparse}), builds the tensor at no extra cost; this degradation is what the
substitution rule of \S\ref{sec:bench-setup} avoids.

\texttt{diffengine} is fastest on seven of the eight problems, with a geometric mean of
$0.48$, that is, $2.1\times$ faster than the best \texttt{CVXPY} backend. The two dashes
are the two problems on which pinned \texttt{cvxcore} does not complete; their ratios,
taken against \texttt{SCIPY} and \texttt{COO}, are upper bounds on
\texttt{diffengine}'s advantage.

clearly beaten, and the cause is a sparse materialization defect rather than derivative
computation; it is discussed in \S\ref{sec:Limitations}.

A parametric problem is re-solved as its parameter values change. After a parameter
update \texttt{CVXPY} re-applies the cached parameter-to-data tensor, while
\texttt{diffengine} re-runs the numeric pass on its cached program;
Table~\ref{tab:param-resolve} compares the two. \texttt{diffengine} re-solves faster on
three of the five problems where both were measured; the tensor is about twice as fast on
the two problems whose parameters enter as small dense maps.

The dashes have two causes. \textsc{ConvexPlasticity} has no tensor to re-apply, because
it is not DPP as the solving chain evaluates it, so \texttt{CVXPY} substitutes the
parameter values and rebuilds from scratch on every re-solve. On
\textsc{SimpleFullyParametrizedLP} and \textsc{ParametrizedQP} the tensor exists, but the
re-solve measurement did not complete on unmodified \texttt{CVXPY}.
\texttt{diffengine} re-solves all three.
