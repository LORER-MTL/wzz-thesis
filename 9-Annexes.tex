\section{Additional problem families}\label{app:extra-problems}

The experiments of \S\ref{sec:bench-nondpp} and \S\ref{sec:bench-sensitivity} use four
problem families beyond the suite. Each is generated from a size and a random seed, with
standard normal data unless stated otherwise.

The \texttt{lasso} family minimizes
\BEQ\label{eq:lasso-family}
\lVert \mmat{A}\mvec{x} - \mvec{b} \rVert_2^2 + \lambda \lVert \mvec{x} \rVert_1
\EEQ
over $\mvec{x} \in \reals^n$, with $\mmat{A} \in \reals^{2n \times n}$. In the sensitivity
experiment, a fraction $1 - \rho$ of the entries of $\mmat{A}$ is set to zero, with
$n \in \{100, 300, 1000, 3000\}$ and $\rho \in \{0.01, 0.05, 0.2, 0.5, 1\}$. In the
\ac{DPP} variant, $\mvec{b}$ and $\lambda$ are parameters. In the non-\ac{DPP} variant,
$\lambda$ is replaced by the product $\alpha\lambda$ of two parameters, which \ac{DPP}
forbids, with $\alpha = 1$. In the parameter-free variant, both are constants.

The \texttt{factor\_cov} family is FactorCovarianceModel with $n$ assets and
$\max(\lfloor n/100 \rfloor, 2)$ factors. Its risk aversion $\gamma$ is a parameter in the
\ac{DPP} variant, the product $\gamma\delta$ of two parameters in the non-\ac{DPP} variant,
and a constant in the parameter-free variant. The \texttt{huber} and \texttt{advertising}
families are HuberRegression with $n$ features and $1.5n$ samples, and OptimalAdvertising
with $n$ time slots and $\max(\lfloor n/4 \rfloor, 2)$ advertisers, both parameter-free.
The scaling experiment uses $n \in \{300, 1000, 3000, 6000\}$ for \texttt{huber},
$n \in \{200, 500, 1000, 2000\}$ for \texttt{advertising}, and
$n \in \{1000, 5000, 25\,000, 50\,000\}$ for \texttt{factor\_cov}, each with five seeds.

\Annexe{EXPERIMENTAL PROTOCOL}\label{app:protocol}

\textbf{Environment.} \tbd{Apple M2, XX GB of memory, macOS XX, Python XX, NumPy XX, SciPy
XX, Clarabel XX}. The \texttt{CVXPY} backends run on the released \texttt{CVXPY}~1.9.2.
\texttt{diffengine} runs on the branch \texttt{thesis-ablation} of \texttt{CVXPY}, at commit
\tbd{XX}, with \texttt{SparseDiffEngine} at commit \tbd{64f7432}, built against Apple's
Accelerate framework. The number of OpenMP and \ac{BLAS} threads is set to one through
four environment variables:
\begin{center}
\small\texttt{OMP\_NUM\_THREADS}, \texttt{OPENBLAS\_NUM\_THREADS}, \texttt{MKL\_NUM\_THREADS},
\texttt{VECLIB\_MAXIMUM\_THREADS}.
\end{center}
The machine runs on mains power with no other application open.

\textbf{Timing.} Each replicate is a new Python process that builds the problem, assigns its
parameters, and times one call to \texttt{get\_problem\_data} with \texttt{time.perf\_counter}.
The process is killed after $30$ minutes. A measurement has $20$ replicates, or $5$ when the
first one exceeds $30$ seconds. Replicate $k$ draws its parameter values from seed $k$, so
the parametric problems change instance from one replicate to the next. A re-solve
replicate runs one first canonicalization, then five parameter updates, each followed by a
timed call, and records the median of the five.

\textbf{Phases.} The branch times the phases of \texttt{diffengine} with wall-clock timers
around the conversion of the expressions, the symbolic pass, the numeric pass, the assembly
of the compressed sparse column matrices, and the reordering of the cone rows. The front end
is the rest of the call.

\textbf{Ablation switches.} With \texttt{DIFFENGINE\_NO\_DENSE=1}, the converters of the
matrix product and of the quadratic form pass every constant matrix to the engine in the
compressed sparse row format, so no permuted dense block is built from constant data.
Parametric matrices keep the dense format. With \texttt{DIFFENGINE\_REBUILD=1}, every
parameter update discards the engine's problem and builds it again, repeating the
conversion and the symbolic pass before the numeric pass.

\textbf{Memory.} Peak memory is measured in a separate process for each problem and
backend, as the growth of the peak resident set size during the call, after a garbage
collection. On a re-solve, the measurement covers the five updates after the first
canonicalization.

\textbf{Correctness.} For each problem, the problem data $(\mmat{P}, \mvec{c}, \mmat{A},
\mvec{b})$ of \texttt{diffengine}, of its two ablation variants and of \texttt{SCIPY} and
\texttt{COO} is compared with that of \texttt{CPP} at the same parameter values. Explicit
zeros are removed first. Two results are equal when the largest absolute difference is at
most $10^{-9}$ times the largest magnitude of the reference, or $10^{-9}$ when that
magnitude is below one. Parametric problems are checked twice, at the first
canonicalization and after a parameter update. Table~\ref{tab:verify} gives the result.

\section{SDPSegfault1132}

Listing~\ref{lst:sdpsegfault} shows the problem, shortened from the benchmark suite. The
variable $\mmat{G} \in \symm^{99}$ is a positive semidefinite Gram matrix, and the matrices
$\mmat{V} \in \reals^{100 \times 99}$, $\mmat{D}$ and $\mmat{W}$ are dense constants.
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python, showstringspaces=false, caption={The SDPSegfault1132
problem, shortened.}, label=lst:sdpsegfault]
n = 100
V = ...                      # dense, (n, n-1)
D = ...                      # dense, (n, n)
W = np.ones((n, n))          # dense, (n, n)

G = cp.Variable((n-1, n-1), PSD=True)
A = cp.kron(e,   cp.reshape(cp.diag(V @ G @ V.T), (1, n)))
B = cp.kron(e.T, cp.reshape(cp.diag(V @ G @ V.T), (n, 1)))
C = cp.norm(cp.multiply(W, A + B - 2 * V @ G @ V.T - D), "fro")

prob = cp.Problem(cp.Maximize(cp.trace(G) - C))
\end{lstlisting}
The map $\mmat{G} \mapsto \mmat{V}\mmat{G}\mmat{V}^\top$ has the dense Jacobian
$\mmat{V} \otimes \mmat{V}$, restricted to the $4950$ free entries of the symmetric
$\mmat{G}$, so it has $10\,000 \times 4950 = 49.5$ million entries. The expression uses it
three times, once directly and twice through the diagonal in $\mmat{A}$ and $\mmat{B}$. In
the sum $\mmat{A} + \mmat{B} - 2\mmat{V}\mmat{G}\mmat{V}^\top$, almost all of these entries
cancel. The entry $(i, j)$ of the sum is $K_{ii} + K_{jj} - 2K_{ij}$ with
$\mmat{K} = \mmat{V}\mmat{G}\mmat{V}^\top$, the identity that turns a Gram matrix into a
matrix of squared distances. Only $1.01$ million nonzeros are left. No backend can know
this in advance, so every backend builds the three dense intermediates.

\texttt{SCIPY} builds them as compressed sparse row matrices, and its sparse addition drops
the entries that sum to zero, so the result of the sum has $1.01$ million entries. The
permuted dense blocks of \texttt{diffengine} have a fixed pattern, so the sum keeps the
$49.5$ million entries, of which $48.5$ million are zero, until the assembly removes them.
% CHECK on the final build: the fork's assembly phase drops the zeros (keep = vals != 0).
% The 2026-08-22 profile (development build) had them dropped only in the cone formatting.
Table~\ref{tab:sdpsegfault-phases} compares where the time goes in the two backends.
On one dense intermediate alone, \texttt{diffengine} is faster, \tbd{$4.1$} seconds against
\tbd{$15.7$}. Its loss is in carrying the cancelled entries through the sum. Removing exact
zeros as soon as a sum produces them would fix this, at the cost of a sparsity pattern that
depends on the values.

\section{ConvexPlasticity}

ConvexPlasticity computes one step of a plasticity model at $N = 3000$ points. Its
objective contains the term
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python, showstringspaces=false]
D = H * np.eye(N)            # diagonal content, dense array
... + 0.5 * cp.quad_form(p - p_old, D)
\end{lstlisting}
The matrix $\mmat{D}$ is diagonal, but it is given as a dense array. The converter of the
quadratic form chose its representation from the type of the array rather than from its
content, so $\mmat{D}$ became a dense $3000 \times 3000$ block of the Hessian. The matrix
$\mmat{P}$ then stored $9.05$ million entries, where its true pattern has about $33$
thousand. The fix applies the same density test as the converter of the matrix product. A
constant with fewer than $5\,\%$ nonzeros takes the sparse representation. With the fix,
the time of \texttt{diffengine} on this problem fell from \tbd{$2.03$} to \tbd{$0.85$}
seconds. The three \texttt{CVXPY} backends take the same \tbd{$0.35$} seconds, which suggests
that this time is spent in the front end they share. The time \texttt{diffengine} spends
above it is \tbd{spent in XX, according to the phases of Table~\ref{tab:phases}}.
