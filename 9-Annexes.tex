\section{Additional problem families}\label{app:extra-problems}

The experiments of \S\ref{sec:bench-nondpp} and \S\ref{sec:bench-sensitivity} use two
problem families beyond the suite, and rescaled versions of the suite problems. Each family
is generated from a size and a random seed, with standard normal data unless stated
otherwise.

The \texttt{lasso} family minimizes
\BEQ\label{eq:lasso-family}
\lVert \mmat{A}\mvec{x} - \mvec{b} \rVert_2^2 + \lambda \lVert \mvec{x} \rVert_1
\EEQ
over $\mvec{x} \in \reals^n$, with $\mmat{A} \in \reals^{2n \times n}$. In the \ac{DPP}
variant, $\mvec{b}$ and $\lambda$ are parameters. In the non-\ac{DPP} variant,
$\lambda$ is replaced by the product $\alpha\lambda$ of two parameters, which \ac{DPP}
forbids, with $\alpha = 1$. In the parameter-free variant, both are constants.
The \texttt{factor\_cov} family is FactorCovarianceModel with $n$ assets and
$\max(\lfloor n/100 \rfloor, 2)$ factors. Its risk aversion $\gamma$ is a parameter in the
\ac{DPP} variant, the product $\gamma\delta$ of two parameters in the non-\ac{DPP} variant,
and a constant in the parameter-free variant.

The sensitivity experiment rescales the problems of the suite. Each problem fixes its sizes
as literals in its source, and the rescaled version multiplies these literals by a factor
$s$ before the problem is built, so that its data has about $s$ times as many nonzeros. All
$25$ problems are rescaled with $s \in \{1/2, 2\}$. Nine of them, HuberRegression,
FactorCovarianceModel, OptimalAdvertising, SVMWithL1Regularization, LeastSquares, SimpleQP,
Yitzhaki, CVaR and SemidefiniteProgramming, are also rescaled with $s \in \{1/4, 4\}$, and
the parameter-free \texttt{lasso} family is measured at $n \in \{250, 500, 1000, 2000,
4000\}$. For the density experiment, the data matrix of LeastSquares, HuberRegression,
SVMWithL1Regularization and SimpleQP, and the matrix $\mmat{A}$ of \texttt{lasso} with
$n = 1000$, is multiplied entrywise by a random $0/1$ mask with a fraction $\rho$ of ones,
drawn from its own generator so that the other data is unchanged. The masked matrix stays a
dense array.

\Annexe{EXPERIMENTAL PROTOCOL}\label{app:protocol}

\textbf{Environment.} The machine is an Apple M2 with $24$~\si{\giga\byte} of memory,
under macOS~26.5.1, with Python~3.13.5, NumPy~2.5.3, SciPy~1.18.1 and
\texttt{Clarabel}~0.11.1. The \texttt{CVXPY}
backends run on the released \texttt{CVXPY}~1.9.2. \texttt{diffengine} runs on the branch
\texttt{thesis-ablation-pr3524} of \texttt{CVXPY}, at commit \texttt{5c07151}, with
\texttt{SparseDiffEngine} at commit \texttt{4f42e05}, built against Apple's Accelerate
framework. The commit \texttt{5c07151} adds the rewrite of \texttt{CVXPY} pull
request~3524 to its parent \texttt{35e81b1}, on which Murray, Cajas,
SemidefiniteProgramming, TvInpainting and the families of \S\ref{sec:bench-nondpp} were
measured. The number of OpenMP and \ac{BLAS} threads is set to one through
four environment variables:
\begin{center}
\small\texttt{OMP\_NUM\_THREADS}, \texttt{OPENBLAS\_NUM\_THREADS}, \texttt{MKL\_NUM\_THREADS},
\texttt{VECLIB\_MAXIMUM\_THREADS}.
\end{center}
The machine runs on mains power with no other application open.

\textbf{Timing.} Each replicate is a new Python process that builds the problem, assigns its
parameters, and times one call to \texttt{get\_problem\_data} with \texttt{time.perf\_counter}.
The process is killed after $30$ minutes, or when its memory exceeds
$20$~\si{\giga\byte}. A measurement has $20$ replicates, or $10$ when the first one exceeds
$30$ seconds, which none did. Replicate $k$ draws its parameter values from seed $k$, so
the parametric problems change instance from one replicate to the next. A re-solve
replicate runs one first canonicalization, then five parameter updates, each followed by a
timed call, and records the median of the five.

\textbf{Rewrite of the \texttt{CVXPY} backends.} On UnconstrainedQP,
QuantumHilbertMatrix, SDPSegfault1132, OptimalAdvertising and SlowPruning, the five problems
on which it applies, the rewrite of pull request~3524 is also applied to the problem before
\texttt{CPP}, \texttt{SCIPY} and \texttt{COO} canonicalize it, inside the timed call. It
takes less than $2$ milliseconds. The problem data of the rewritten problem is checked
against that of \texttt{CPP} on the original problem, with the test below.

\textbf{Phases.} The branch times the phases of \texttt{diffengine} with wall-clock timers
around the conversion of the expressions, the symbolic pass, the numeric pass, the assembly
of the compressed sparse column matrices, and the reordering of the cone rows. The front end
is the rest of the call.

\textbf{Ablation switches.} With \texttt{DIFFENGINE\_NO\_DENSE=1}, the converters of the
matrix product and of the quadratic form pass every constant matrix to the engine in the
compressed sparse row format, so no permuted dense block is built from constant data.
Parametric matrices keep the dense format. The fully sparse variant of
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} sets the same switch and evaluates the parameters before the
canonicalization, so that every matrix is constant. With \texttt{DIFFENGINE\_REBUILD=1}, every
parameter update discards the engine's problem and builds it again, repeating the
conversion and the symbolic pass before the numeric pass.

\textbf{Memory.} Peak memory is measured in a separate process for each problem and
backend, as the growth of the peak resident set size during the call, after a garbage
collection. On a re-solve, the measurement covers the five updates after the first
canonicalization.

\textbf{Correctness.} For each problem, the problem data $(\mmat{P}, \mvec{c}, \mmat{A},
\mvec{b})$ of \texttt{diffengine}, of its ablation variants and of \texttt{SCIPY} and
\texttt{COO} is compared with that of \texttt{CPP} at the same parameter values. On a
parametric problem, the reference is \texttt{CPP} on the problem with its parameters
evaluated. Explicit
zeros are removed first. Two results are equal when the largest absolute difference is at
most $10^{-9}$ times the largest magnitude of the reference, or $10^{-9}$ when that
magnitude is below one. Parametric problems are checked twice, at the first
canonicalization and after a parameter update. Table~\ref{tab:verify} gives the result. The
rescaled problems of \S\ref{sec:bench-sensitivity} are checked in the same way. On seven of
them, the check needs more than $20$~\si{\giga\byte}, so their times are not reported.

\Annexe{TWO SLOWER PROBLEMS}\label{app:cases}

This appendix describes the two problems of \S\ref{sec:bench-phases} on which dense
constant data slows \texttt{diffengine} down.

\section{SDPSegfault1132}

Listing~\ref{lst:sdpsegfault} shows the problem, shortened from the benchmark suite. The
variable $\mmat{G} \in \symm^{99}$ is a positive semidefinite Gram matrix, and the matrices
$\mmat{V} \in \reals^{100 \times 99}$, $\mmat{D}$ and $\mmat{W}$ are dense constants.
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python, showstringspaces=false, caption={The SDPSegfault1132
problem, shortened.}, label=lst:sdpsegfault]
n = 100
V = ...                      # dense, (n, n-1)
D = ...                      # dense, (n, n)
W = np.ones((n, n))          # dense, (n, n)

G = cp.Variable((n-1, n-1), PSD=True)
A = cp.kron(e,   cp.reshape(cp.diag(V @ G @ V.T), (1, n)))
B = cp.kron(e.T, cp.reshape(cp.diag(V @ G @ V.T), (n, 1)))
C = cp.norm(cp.multiply(W, A + B - 2 * V @ G @ V.T - D), "fro")

prob = cp.Problem(cp.Maximize(cp.trace(G) - C))
\end{lstlisting}
The map $\mmat{G} \mapsto \mmat{V}\mmat{G}\mmat{V}^\top$ has the dense Jacobian
$\mmat{V} \otimes \mmat{V}$, restricted to the $4950$ free entries of the symmetric
$\mmat{G}$, so it has $10\,000 \times 4950 = 49.5$ million entries. The expression uses it
three times, once directly and twice through the diagonal in $\mmat{A}$ and $\mmat{B}$. In
the sum $\mmat{A} + \mmat{B} - 2\mmat{V}\mmat{G}\mmat{V}^\top$, almost all of these entries
cancel. The entry $(i, j)$ of the sum is $K_{ii} + K_{jj} - 2K_{ij}$ with
$\mmat{K} = \mmat{V}\mmat{G}\mmat{V}^\top$, the identity that turns a Gram matrix into a
matrix of squared distances. Only $1.01$ million nonzeros are left. No backend can know
this in advance, so every backend builds the three dense intermediates.

\texttt{SCIPY} builds them as compressed sparse row matrices, and its sparse addition drops
the entries that sum to zero, so the result of the sum has $1.01$ million entries. The
permuted dense blocks of \texttt{diffengine} have a fixed pattern, so the sum keeps the
$49.5$ million entries, of which $48.5$ million are zero, until the assembly removes them.
Table~\ref{tab:sdpsegfault-phases} shows where the time of \texttt{diffengine} goes. The
symbolic and numeric passes, which carry the cancelled entries, take $1.82$ of its
$1.90$ seconds, against $1.51$ seconds for the whole canonicalization of \texttt{SCIPY}. The
dense blocks themselves are not the cause. Without them, \texttt{diffengine} takes
$3.29$ seconds, because its sparse sum keeps the cancelled entries as well. Removing exact
zeros as soon as a sum produces them would fix this, at the cost of a sparsity pattern that
depends on the values.

\section{ConvexPlasticity}

ConvexPlasticity computes one step of a plasticity model at $N = 3000$ points. Its
objective contains the term
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python, showstringspaces=false]
D = H * np.eye(N)            # diagonal content, dense array
... + 0.5 * cp.quad_form(p - p_old, D)
\end{lstlisting}
The matrix $\mmat{D}$ is diagonal, but it is given as a dense array. On a parameter-free
problem, the converter of the quadratic form applies the same density test as the converter
of the matrix product, and a constant with fewer than $5\,\%$ nonzeros takes the sparse
representation. ConvexPlasticity has three parameters, and its quadratic form takes the
route that keeps them symbolic, on which the test is not applied. $\mmat{D}$ therefore
becomes a dense $3000 \times 3000$ block of the Hessian, and the matrix $\mmat{P}$ stores
$9.05$ million entries, where its true pattern has about $33$ thousand.
% CHECK: CHANGES.md (NO_DENSE row) says constant P in the symbolic quad_form uses CSR only
% under DIFFENGINE_NO_DENSE=1; confirm that this is the route ConvexPlasticity takes.
\texttt{diffengine} takes $0.50$ seconds, of which the symbolic pass and the assembly take
$85\,\%$, and its peak memory grows by $1.3$~\si{\giga\byte}. The three \texttt{CVXPY}
backends take $0.072$ to $0.079$ seconds and grow by $82$~\si{\mega\byte}. With the dense
representations off, $\mmat{D}$ is stored in the sparse format, and \texttt{diffengine}
takes $0.098$ seconds and grows by $26$~\si{\mega\byte}. Applying the density test on the
symbolic route as well would close most of the gap.
