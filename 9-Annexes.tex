\Annexe{BENCHMARK PROBLEMS}\label{app:bench}

Table~\ref{tab:instances-desc} describes the $25$ problems of the benchmark
suite~\cite{cvxpybenchmarks} used in Chapter~\ref{sec:Theme2}, and
Table~\ref{tab:instances} gives their dimensions, both in the order of
Table~\ref{tab:oneshot-compile}. The dimensions are those of the problem data that
\texttt{diffengine} returns for \texttt{Clarabel}: the matrix
$\mmat{A} \in \reals^{m \times n}$ of the constraints and the matrix $\mmat{P}$ of the
objective, both counted after canonicalization, so $n$ includes the auxiliary variables of
the reductions. The density is the share of nonzero entries of $\mmat{A}$,
$\mathrm{nnz}(\mmat{A})/(mn)$, the column ``param.\ size'' is the total number of entries of
the parameters of the problem, and the last column says whether the problem is \ac{DPP}. A dash
denotes a problem without a quadratic objective or without parameters.

\Annexe{SENSITIVITY TO SIZE AND DENSITY}\label{app:sweeps}

% Ratios from results/thesis-n30/grid_facts.json in canonicalization-benchmarks
% (thesis_figures_n30.py); the s = 1 column matches Table 4.1.
This appendix details the sweeps of \S\ref{sec:bench-sensitivity}. Every problem, and the
\texttt{lasso} of \S\ref{sec:bench-nondpp} without parameters, is scaled by a factor $s$ of its
size literals, which multiplies the nonzeros of its data by about $s$. Points whose first run
exceeds $30$~seconds are made in $10$ runs, the others in $30$. Table~\ref{tab:app-size} gives
the ratio of \texttt{diffengine} to the best \texttt{CVXPY} backend. Over a factor of $16$ in
size, it stays within $20\,\%$ of its value at $s = 1$ on $18$ of the $22$ problems where
\texttt{diffengine} is faster. On three of the four slower problems, the gap widens with the
size, for the reasons given in \S\ref{sec:bench-oneshot}.

For the density, the data matrix of eight problems is multiplied entrywise by a random $0/1$
mask with a fraction $\rho$ of ones, and kept as a dense array, as in a user's code.
Table~\ref{tab:app-density} gives the ratio with and without the dense blocks. With them, the
ratio is lower on dense data but rises faster as $\rho$ falls, because a dense block stores
every entry, zeros included. The two cross between $\rho = 0.5$ and $\rho = 0.2$, except on
SemidefiniteProgramming, where the dense blocks make no difference. Without them,
\texttt{diffengine} stays faster down to $\rho = 0.01$ on six of the eight problems.
